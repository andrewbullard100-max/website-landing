What Crunchtime (Zenput) actually is

Crunchtime is a back-office restaurant management platform built for large chains, covering labor scheduling, food cost management, and inventory. In 2022, Crunchtime acquired Zenput, which had been an operations-execution platform specifically focused on digital checklists, audit management, and temperature monitoring for multi-unit foodservice. The combined product is now marketed as Crunchtime Ops Execution.

For most buyers researching this comparison, "Crunchtime" in the context of food safety and compliance means the former Zenput product: task management, digital audits pushed from a central operations team to the field, and compliance tracking sold to large QSR and casual-dining chains. Zenput built a significant customer base among national chains because it integrated cleanly with an enterprise operations workflow: assign tasks to specific locations, push required checklists out to the field, track completion rates, and review audit results from a central dashboard.

It is a well-established tool for enterprise operations teams, and it fits into a formal procurement process that those teams already know how to run. The tradeoffs relative to FoodSafe IQ are primarily about scale, selling model, and what "violation detection" actually means in each system.

Where each platform fits well

Crunchtime tends to fit well when

  • You're running 100+ locations with a dedicated operations or compliance team
  • You already use Crunchtime's back-office platform and want to consolidate tools
  • You have a formal RFP and procurement process and need an enterprise vendor relationship
  • You need custom audit configurations built around your chain's specific processes
  • Your compliance team manages audit cadences, performance benchmarks, and vendor contracts

FoodSafe IQ tends to fit well when

  • You're in the 5 to 50 location range and want a real compliance program without enterprise overhead
  • You want AI reviewing actual photos of each kitchen rather than assigning digital checklists
  • You want to start a free trial today rather than schedule a discovery call
  • Published pricing that you can budget with before any sales engagement matters
  • Inspection readiness is the specific outcome you're solving for, not general ops execution

The core tradeoff

The fundamental difference between these two platforms is scale and selling model, not product quality. Crunchtime Ops Execution (formerly Zenput) is a serious, capable platform that earned its position in the large-chain market. The issue for a mid-market operator isn't whether it works. It's whether the enterprise sales process, custom implementation timelines, and custom pricing model match what they're actually trying to buy.

Operators with 8 to 30 locations are typically not buying through a formal procurement process. They're trying to solve a specific problem: "our regional manager can't be in every kitchen, and we don't know what condition each one is in until something goes wrong." FoodSafe IQ is built for exactly that scope and that decision-making process.

The verification question worth asking: a checklist completed by a kitchen employee tells you someone went through the motions. A photo reviewed by AI tells you what the kitchen actually looked like when the checklist was done. That's the specific claim FoodSafe IQ makes, and it's the gap that matters most for operators whose core concern is what an inspector sees on a random Tuesday afternoon.

The corrective action loop is also worth comparing. Both platforms have some form of corrective action tracking. FoodSafe IQ requires a named owner, a due date, and a verification step before a finding can be closed. The verification requirement is the part that matters most for multi-unit programs: it closes the failure mode where a manager marks something complete without anyone confirming the fix actually happened.

Pricing in plain terms

Crunchtime does not publish pricing for its Ops Execution product. It's sold through a sales engagement, and pricing is custom based on chain size, modules selected, and contract terms. Independent buyers report that enterprise implementations at this level typically require a multi-month procurement and onboarding process before a location goes live.

FoodSafe IQ's pricing is on its website: $79 per location per month (or $790 per location per year) for one to five locations, $67.15 per location for six to ten, $59.25 for eleven to twenty, and $51.35 for twenty-one to fifty. All features are included at every level. The full pricing table is at foodsafeiq.com/pricing, and a 14-day free trial with no credit card required means you can verify the product fits before committing to anything.

Who should consider each

Consider Crunchtime if you're running a large chain with 100+ locations and a dedicated ops team; you already use Crunchtime's back-office platform; you have a formal vendor procurement process; and you need customized audit workflows configured around your brand's specific standards.

Consider FoodSafe IQ if you're a regional operator in the 5 to 50 location range; you want a food safety program that runs without an enterprise implementation timeline; AI-reviewed photos of actual kitchen conditions matter more than configurable task workflows; and you want to evaluate the product in a free trial rather than a sales presentation.

Both platforms are built for multi-unit food safety compliance. The difference is who they were built for and what kind of buying process they're designed to fit into.
