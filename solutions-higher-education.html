Higher Education Dining

Food safety software built for multi-hall campus dining

Standardize inspections across every dining hall on campus — run largely by student workers who rotate every semester — before the health department or a parent's complaint does.

Why campus dining is a different problem

01
Student worker turnover

Most dining halls are staffed largely by student employees who rotate every semester. A paper checklist system depends on institutional memory that walks out the door every May. A structured 73-point checklist and AI photo review don't depend on who's on shift.

02
Reputational stakes are public

A health department score or an outbreak at a dining hall doesn't stay internal — it becomes a story students, parents, and local press see. Catching violations before an inspector does is protection for the whole institution, not just the dining department's budget line.

03
Multiple halls, one director

Most campuses run several dining halls, cafes, and convenience locations under one dining services umbrella. A single dashboard showing every location's score — instead of a folder of individual PDFs — is what makes oversight actually possible.

Built to fit how dining services actually runs

FoodSafe IQ runs on any phone or tablet — no app install required — so a student worker or shift lead can complete a Quick Scan or full audit on the equipment already in the kitchen. AI reviews photos from each zone (cooler, prep, dish, receiving) against a full inspection checklist, flags potential violations, and recommends specific corrective steps. A manager assigns the owner and due date, preserving clear accountability.

For the dining director or assistant director overseeing multiple halls, every location rolls up into one dashboard — scores, open corrective actions, and trends, without waiting for a stack of reports the morning after a bad audit.
