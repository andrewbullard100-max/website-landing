North Carolina Food Code: A Complete Guide for Restaurant Operators

How NCDHHS inspects restaurants, what the A, B, and C letter grades mean, what score triggers permit revocation, and which violations get cited most often. Everything operators need to stay compliant.

How North Carolina Food Inspections Work

Food safety inspections in North Carolina are conducted by environmental health specialists employed by county health departments, overseen by the North Carolina Department of Health and Human Services (NCDHHS). The state enforces the FDA Food Code 2017 and the 2017 Supplement, adopted through 15A NCAC 18A .2650.

Inspections are unannounced. Most restaurants are inspected at least twice per year, though higher-risk establishments or those with a history of compliance issues may receive additional visits. Complaint-based inspections can occur at any time and do not count as routine inspections.

Inspectors use a 29-item standardized form developed by NCDHHS. Each item is scored, and the total determines the letter grade. Items are divided into weighted categories: foodborne illness risk factors carry more point value than general food sanitation practices or structural compliance issues.

One important feature specific to North Carolina: establishments can earn a 2-point credit on their inspection score if they have a certified food protection manager on site who holds an ANSI-accredited certification (such as ServSafe). This credit applies automatically when the inspector verifies the credential.

After each inspection, the letter grade card must be posted in a location visible to customers at the point of entry. Grade cards are public record and are searchable through the NCDHHS Environmental Health Public Information portal.

Quick Reference

Regulatory authority: NC Department of Health and Human Services (NCDHHS)

North Carolina's Letter Grading System

North Carolina uses a letter grade system based on a 100-point scale. Grades must be posted conspicuously at the restaurant entrance. A permit can be revoked immediately for scores below 70 under G.S. 130A-23(d).

A
Score: 90 to 100
The facility is in substantial compliance with the NC food code. An A grade is the standard expectation for a well-run restaurant. Posted at the entrance -- customers see it when they walk in.
B
Score: 80 to 89.5
Compliance issues exist and must be corrected. A B grade triggers a mandatory follow-up inspection. The facility may remain open, but the B grade card must be posted and is visible to the public.
C
Score: 70 to 79.5
Significant compliance failures. A C grade is a serious result that triggers immediate re-inspection. Customers see the C card posted at entry. Multiple consecutive C grades substantially increase inspection frequency.
Revoke
Score: below 70
Under G.S. 130A-23(d), scoring below 70 is grounds for immediate permit revocation. The facility cannot legally operate until a re-inspection demonstrates compliance and the permit is reinstated.

Most Common Violations in North Carolina Restaurant Inspections

These are the categories where NC inspectors most frequently cite violations. Addressing these proactively is the fastest path to a consistent A grade.

01
Temperature control for TCS foodsFoods held cold above 41°F or hot below 135°F. The 2017 Food Code and its supplement reinforce the FDA's risk-based approach to temperature control. Check coolers, prep tables, and steam tables at the start of every shift and document the readings.
02
Handwashing procedures and bare-hand contactEmployees not washing hands at required intervals, or making bare-hand contact with ready-to-eat foods. NC county inspectors observe handwashing practices during the inspection. Bare-hand contact with RTE foods is a critical violation.
03
Food storage and cross-contaminationRaw proteins stored above ready-to-eat foods, or TCS foods stored without protection from contamination. Walk-in organization is inspected on every visit. A single misplaced tray of raw chicken above a pan of ready-to-eat food is a critical citation.
04
Date marking and discardReady-to-eat TCS foods prepared on site must be date-marked if held longer than 24 hours and discarded no later than 7 days after preparation (when stored at 41°F or below). Missing labels and overdue items are among the most common critical violations found in NC inspections.
05
Certified food protection manager absenceNorth Carolina requires at least one certified food protection manager per establishment. The 2-point credit only applies when that person is present during the inspection. Absence at inspection time means no credit and a potential deduction.
06
Equipment and surface sanitationFood contact surfaces not sanitized at the correct concentration or frequency. NC inspectors test sanitizer solutions with test strips and check warewasher rinse temperatures. Inadequate sanitizer concentration is a point deduction on every inspection form.
