Kentucky restaurant inspections: scoring, violations, and compliance

How the Kentucky CHFS Food Safety Branch inspects restaurants under 902 KAR 45:005, what Green and Red placards mean, and which violations get cited most often. Everything you need to stay ready for an unannounced visit.

How Kentucky Food Inspections Work

Food safety inspections in Kentucky are conducted by the Kentucky Cabinet for Health and Family Services (CHFS), Food Safety Branch. The Branch enforces the FDA Food Code 2013, adopted by reference under 902 KAR 45:005 §9(1)(a) (effective February 21, 2019; recertified October 13, 2025), making it the regulatory foundation for every restaurant inspection in the state.

Inspections are unannounced and occur 1 to 4 times per year depending on a facility's risk category. Higher-risk operations -- those handling raw proteins, serving vulnerable populations such as nursing home and senior living dining programs, or with a history of violations -- are inspected more frequently than lower-risk facilities.

Inspectors use Form DFS-208, a 30-item checklist organized around the major risk factors the FDA Food Code 2013 identifies as most likely to cause foodborne illness: employee health and hygiene, temperature control for safety (TCS) foods, cross-contamination prevention, chemical management, and facility sanitation. Each item is weighted, and priority items carry greater scoring impact than priority foundation and core items.

After an inspection, the establishment receives a written report citing each violation by the specific section of 902 KAR 45:005 it violates. Priority violations must be corrected within 10 days regardless of the overall score. Non-critical corrections may have longer timelines depending on the score range.

Kentucky's Inspection Scoring and Placard System

Kentucky uses a 100-point numerical score. Points are deducted for each violation found, weighted by severity. The score determines which placard is posted and what correction timelines apply. There are two placard colors: Green and Red.

Green

Score: 85 or above, no priority violations
The establishment is in substantial compliance with the Kentucky food code. The Green placard must be posted where customers can see it. Non-critical violations identified during the inspection must be corrected before the next routine inspection.

Red

Score: 84 or below, OR any priority violation present
A Red placard is posted, signaling compliance issues that require correction. All priority violations must be corrected within 10 days regardless of score. The sub-bands within the Red zone determine further enforcement action:

Correction timelines within Red

  • Score 70 to 84: Non-critical violations corrected within 30 days; re-inspection scheduled
  • Score below 70: DFS-214 Notice of Enforcement issued -- intent to suspend the operating permit
  • Score below 60: Grounds for immediate permit suspension and closure until re-inspection shows compliance

Important note on critical vs. non-critical violations: The FDA Food Code 2013 (adopted by KY) classifies items as priority (most likely to cause foodborne illness), priority foundation (supports priority items), and core (general sanitation). Any priority violation triggers a Red placard regardless of the point score -- a restaurant with a 92 score that has a bare-hand contact violation still receives a Red placard until that violation is corrected.

Governing Code and State-Specific Rules

Kentucky enforces the FDA Food Code 2013 by direct adoption under 902 KAR 45:005. The 2013 edition is the currently adopted version in Kentucky as of the most recent recertification on October 13, 2025. This matters for operators comparing requirements across states: some neighboring states have since adopted the 2017 or 2022 Food Code editions, which differ in some provisions.

Kentucky's real inspection form (DFS-208) has 30 items, which does not map one-to-one to the FDA Food Code's model structure. The items are organized by risk-factor category, and the form's hot-holding and cold-holding items appear in reverse order compared to the FDA model -- a practical note for operators reviewing the form alongside federal guidance.

Certified food protection manager (CFPM) requirement: Under the FDA Food Code 2013, Section 2-102.12 (adopted by reference via 902 KAR 45:005), each food establishment must have at least one employee who has supervisory and management responsibility and holds a valid CFPM certification from an ANSI-accredited program such as ServSafe or NRFSP. If the person on duty cannot produce valid certification, it is a violation.

Most Common Violations in Kentucky Restaurant Inspections

These are the categories where Kentucky inspectors most frequently find violations. Citations below reference 902 KAR 45:005, which adopts the FDA Food Code 2013 by reference. Most of these violations are preventable with consistent daily practice and documentation.

01
Improper temperature control for TCS foodsCold-held foods found above 41°F or hot-held foods found below 135°F. 902 KAR 45:005 (FDA Food Code 2013 §§3-501.16(A)(1) and (A)(2)). Common causes include overfilled prep tables, walk-in doors left open, or equipment needing calibration. Temperature logs documenting readings across the shift are the primary defense.
02
Handwashing and bare-hand contact with RTE foodsEmployees not washing hands at required intervals or making bare-hand contact with ready-to-eat foods. 902 KAR 45:005 (FDA Food Code 2013 §§2-301.12, 2-301.14, 3-301.11). Both are priority violations and trigger a Red placard regardless of overall score.
03
Cross-contamination in food storageRaw proteins stored above or adjacent to ready-to-eat foods in the walk-in. 902 KAR 45:005 (FDA Food Code 2013 §3-302.11). The correct order from top to bottom: ready-to-eat foods, seafood, whole-cut meats, ground meats, poultry. Any deviation is a priority citation.
04
Date labeling and discardPrepared foods without a use-by date or foods held past their 7-day maximum when stored at 41°F or below. 902 KAR 45:005 (FDA Food Code 2013 §§3-501.17, 3-501.18). Inspectors check every label in the cooler. Unlabeled or overdue items are a priority citation.
05
Sanitizer concentration out of rangeChlorine sanitizer below 50 ppm or above 200 ppm, or quaternary ammonium sanitizer outside the manufacturer's required range. 902 KAR 45:005 (FDA Food Code 2013 §4-501.114). Test strips should be used every time a sanitizer bucket is prepared, with results logged.
06
Certified food protection manager not present or certifiedKentucky requires at least one certified food protection manager per establishment under 902 KAR 45:005 (FDA Food Code 2013 §2-102.12). If the manager on duty cannot produce a valid ANSI-accredited certification (such as ServSafe or NRFSP), it is a violation. Credentials must be current and verifiable.

How to Find Kentucky Restaurant Inspection Records

Kentucky inspection results are available to the public through the CHFS Food Protection online portal at foodprotection.ky.gov. You can search by establishment name, permit number, county, or address. Each inspection report includes the score, the placard color issued, and a list of every violation cited with the specific code section.

Complaint-based inspections are also recorded in the portal. Establishments may request copies of their inspection reports directly from their local district health department or through the CHFS Food Safety Branch.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often are restaurants inspected in Kentucky?
Kentucky restaurants are inspected 1 to 4 times per year depending on their risk category. Higher-risk operations that handle raw proteins, serve vulnerable populations, or have a history of violations are inspected more frequently. All routine inspections are unannounced and conducted by the CHFS Food Safety Branch.
What does a Green placard mean in Kentucky?
A Green placard means the establishment scored 85 or above on its most recent inspection and had no priority violations. The placard is issued by the Kentucky CHFS Food Safety Branch and must be posted where customers can see it. Non-critical violations found during that inspection must still be corrected before the next routine visit.
What triggers a Red placard in Kentucky?
A Red placard is issued when an establishment scores 84 or below, or when any priority violation is found during the inspection -- regardless of the overall score. Facilities with a Red placard must correct all priority violations within 10 days. A score below 70 triggers a DFS-214 Notice of Enforcement (intent to suspend the permit), and a score below 60 is grounds for immediate permit suspension.
What is the DFS-208 form?
The DFS-208 is the official Kentucky food service establishment inspection form used by CHFS Food Safety Branch inspectors. It has 30 items organized around the major risk factors in the FDA Food Code 2013: employee health and hygiene, temperature control for safety foods, cross-contamination prevention, chemical management, and facility sanitation. Each item is weighted, with priority items carrying more scoring impact.
