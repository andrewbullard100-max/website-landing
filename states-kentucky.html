Kentucky Food Code: A Complete Guide for Restaurant Operators

How Kentucky inspects restaurants, what the DFS-208 form covers, what Green and Red placards mean, and which violations get cited most often. Everything you need to stay ready for an unannounced visit.

How Kentucky Food Inspections Work

Food safety inspections in Kentucky are conducted by the Kentucky Cabinet for Health and Family Services (CHFS), Food Safety Branch. The Branch enforces the FDA Food Code 2013, adopted by reference under 902 KAR 45:005, making it the regulatory foundation for every inspection in the state.

Inspections are unannounced and typically occur one to four times per year depending on a facility's risk category. Higher-risk operations -- those handling raw proteins, serving vulnerable populations, or with a history of violations -- are inspected more frequently than lower-risk facilities like convenience stores or snack bars.

Inspectors use Form DFS-208, a 30-item checklist organized around the major risk factors the FDA Food Code identifies as most likely to cause foodborne illness: employee health and hygiene, temperature control for safety (TCS) foods, cross-contamination prevention, chemical management, and facility sanitation. Each item is weighted, and critical items carry more scoring impact than non-critical items.

After an inspection, the establishment receives a written report citing each violation by the specific section of the Kentucky food code it violates. The facility must correct critical violations before the inspector leaves, or within a very short time frame, while non-critical violations typically have a longer corrective action window.

Quick Reference

Regulatory authority: KY Cabinet for Health and Family Services, Food Safety Branch

Adopted food code: FDA Food Code 2013

Regulation citation: 902 KAR 45:005

Inspection form: DFS-208 (30 items)

Inspection frequency: 1 to 4 times per year, risk-based

Inspections announced: No -- all routine inspections are unannounced

Public results: Available through the CHFS online portal

TCS cold holding: 41°F or below

TCS hot holding: 135°F or above

Kentucky's Inspection Scoring System

Kentucky uses a 100-point numerical score combined with a placard system. The score is calculated based on deductions for each violation found during the inspection, weighted by the severity of the item.

Green
Score: 85 or above, no critical violations
The establishment receives a Green placard. This is the standard passing result and means the facility is operating in substantial compliance with the food code. The placard must be posted where customers can see it.
Follow-Up
Score: 70 to 84
A re-inspection is scheduled within 30 days. The facility may remain open but must correct identified violations before the follow-up visit. Scoring in this range repeatedly increases inspection frequency.
Red
Score: below 84 OR any critical violation
A Red placard is posted, signaling serious compliance issues. The facility must correct critical violations before continuing operation. Red placard status triggers mandatory re-inspection.
Suspend
Score: 60 to 69
The Food Safety Branch issues a notice to suspend the operating license. The facility must take immediate corrective action and pass a re-inspection before the suspension takes effect.
Closure
Score: below 60
Immediate closure. The facility cannot reopen until it passes a re-inspection and the inspector verifies that all violations have been corrected.

Most Common Violations in Kentucky Restaurant Inspections

These are the categories where Kentucky inspectors most frequently find violations. Most of them are preventable with consistent daily practice and documentation.

01
Improper temperature control for TCS foodsCold-held foods found above 41°F, or hot-held foods found below 135°F. Common causes include overfilled prep tables, walk-in doors left open, or equipment that needs calibration. Temperature logs that document readings across the shift are the primary defense.
02
Handwashing and employee hygieneEmployees not washing hands after handling raw proteins, after breaks, or when switching tasks. Inspectors watch handwashing sink usage directly. Bare-hand contact with ready-to-eat foods is a critical violation under the 2013 FDA Food Code.
03
Cross-contamination in food storageRaw proteins stored above or adjacent to ready-to-eat foods in the walk-in. The correct order from top to bottom is: ready-to-eat foods, then seafood, then whole-cut meats, then ground meats, then poultry. Any deviation is a citation.
04
Date labeling and discard compliancePrepared foods without a use-by date, or foods held past their 7-day maximum (41°F or below). Inspectors check every label in the cooler. Unlabeled or overdue items are an automatic critical citation.
05
Sanitizer concentration out of rangeChlorine sanitizer below 50 ppm or above 200 ppm, or quaternary ammonium sanitizer outside the manufacturer's required range. Test strips should be used every time a sanitizer bucket is prepared, with results logged.
06
Certified food protection manager requirementKentucky requires that each food establishment have at least one certified food protection manager. If the person on duty cannot produce a valid certification, it is a violation. ANSI-accredited certifications such as ServSafe are accepted.
