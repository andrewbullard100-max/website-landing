Arkansas restaurant inspections: risk categories and violation codes

Arkansas assigns every food service establishment to a risk category that determines inspection frequency and scrutiny level under Ark. Admin. Code 007.04.21-003. Here is how ADH categorizes operations, what inspectors check, and how violations are cited with specific Arkansas code references.

How Arkansas food inspections work

Food safety inspections in Arkansas are conducted by the Arkansas Department of Health (ADH), Food Protection Program. County offices carry out inspections statewide under state-level oversight. Full results are publicly accessible at healthy.arkansas.gov.

Arkansas assigns every licensed food service establishment to one of three risk categories: Category 1 (Low), Category 2 (Medium), or Category 3 (High). The category is determined by the type of food operations performed, the population served, and the complexity of food handling at the facility. The risk category drives inspection frequency and the level of regulatory scrutiny applied during each visit.

The governing rules are Ark. Admin. Code 007.04.21-003 (Retail Food Establishment Rules), effective October 24, 2022. Arkansas uses its own section numbering -- not the FDA Food Code model ordinance section numbers. Each violation cited by an ADH inspector includes a specific Arkansas code section reference, so corrective action notices must track the actual Arkansas rule provision rather than the FDA Food Code number.

All routine inspections are unannounced. Re-inspections for critical violations or compliance failures are also unannounced.

Arkansas risk categories

ADH classifies every food service establishment by risk level under Ark. Admin. Code 007.04.21-003. The risk category determines inspection frequency and the level of regulatory scrutiny applied during each visit. Most full-service restaurants are Category 2.

Category 1

Low Risk
Operations that handle only commercially pre-packaged foods or do not handle TCS foods. Examples include convenience stores that sell only sealed products or facilities with minimal food handling. Inspected least frequently because the risk of foodborne illness from these operations is lower.

Category 2

Medium Risk -- Most Restaurants
Operations that cook raw animal proteins, hold TCS foods hot or cold, and serve the general public. This category covers the large majority of full-service restaurants, fast food operations, and food trucks in Arkansas. Inspected more frequently than Category 1, with unannounced routine and follow-up visits.

Category 3

High Risk
Operations that serve highly susceptible populations such as nursing home residents, hospital patients, or daycare children, or perform specialized food processes such as smoking, curing, or reduced-oxygen packaging. Inspected most frequently. Compliance requirements are stricter and enforcement responses to violations are more rapid.

Violation types and correction windows

Under Ark. Admin. Code 007.04.21-003, ADH classifies violations by type and assigns correction windows based on risk level:

Priority violations are items directly linked to foodborne illness risk -- improper holding temperatures, inadequate cooking, contamination from raw proteins, employee illness, chemical contamination, and approved source requirements. Priority violations carry a correction window of 4 to 30 days depending on severity. Some priority violations require immediate corrective action on-site during the inspection.

Priority Foundation violations support the conditions that prevent priority violations -- equipment calibration, water temperature, and HACCP plan compliance. Priority Foundation items carry a correction window of 4 to 10 days.

Core violations are items related to general maintenance, facility condition, and good practice requirements not directly linked to immediate illness risk. Core violations carry a correction window of up to 90 days.

Each violation is documented on the inspection report with the specific Ark. Admin. Code 007.04.21-003 section reference. Arkansas section numbers are not a direct match to FDA Food Code section numbers -- always reference the Arkansas code when drafting corrective action plans.

CFPM requirements in Arkansas

Under Ark. Admin. Code 007.04.21-003, the Person in Charge of a food service establishment during each operating shift must be able to demonstrate food safety knowledge to an ADH inspector. This includes knowledge of applicable provisions of the Arkansas rules, foodborne illness risk factors, and the corrective actions required for each type of violation.

Specific Certified Food Protection Manager (CFPM) certification requirements under Arkansas law apply to certain operation types and risk categories. For Category 3 operations serving highly susceptible populations -- including nursing homes and assisted living facilities -- the documentation requirements for food safety training and competency are more stringent. Operators should confirm exact CFPM certification requirements with ADH Food Protection Program directly, as requirements may vary by operation type and local ordinance.

For healthcare and senior living dining operations in Arkansas, federal CMS standards under 42 CFR 483.60 apply independently of state CFPM rules and require documented evidence of food safety competency for dietary staff serving Medicare- and Medicaid-certified residents.

Most common violations in Arkansas restaurant inspections

ADH inspectors cite each violation with a specific Ark. Admin. Code 007.04.21-003 section reference. These are the violation categories most frequently found in Arkansas food service inspections, particularly for Category 2 operations.

01
Temperature control failures (cold and hot holding)TCS foods held above 41°F in cold storage or below 135°F in hot holding are the most frequently cited violations in Arkansas. ADH inspectors probe temperatures directly during every routine visit. Each violation is cited with the specific Arkansas code section addressing temperature control for safety foods.
02
Improper cooling of cooked foodsArkansas follows the two-stage cooling requirement: 135°F to 70°F within 2 hours, then 70°F to 41°F within the next 4 hours. Large batches of rice, beans, soups, and proteins that are not cooled in shallow pans or with ice baths frequently fail this standard. Inspectors check cooling logs and probe foods in coolers.
03
Employee hygiene and handwashingBare-hand contact with ready-to-eat foods, failure to wash hands between raw protein handling and other food prep, and handwashing sinks that are inaccessible or lacking soap and paper towels. Each finding is cited with the specific handwashing or personal hygiene code provision from Ark. Admin. Code 007.04.21-003.
04
Cross-contamination from raw protein storageRaw meats, poultry, or seafood stored above or adjacent to ready-to-eat foods in walk-ins and reach-ins. ADH inspectors check cooler organization on every routine inspection. Violations cite the specific storage hierarchy provision from Arkansas food service rules.
05
Date labeling and discard complianceReady-to-eat TCS foods prepared on site and held beyond 24 hours must carry a preparation date and consume-by date within 7 days. Missing labels, incorrect dates, and foods past their discard date are common findings. Inspectors check every labeled and unlabeled container in refrigerated storage.
06
Sanitizing solution concentration and equipment sanitationChlorine or quaternary ammonium sanitizers outside the required concentration range, and multiuse food-contact surfaces not sanitized between uses. Inspectors test sanitizer solutions during the visit. Each violation cites the specific equipment sanitation or chemical sanitizer provision in Ark. Admin. Code 007.04.21-003.

Frequently asked questions

What risk category are most Arkansas restaurants?

Most full-service restaurants in Arkansas are classified as Category 2 (Medium Risk) under Ark. Admin. Code 007.04.21-003. This includes operations that cook raw animal proteins to order, hold TCS foods, and serve the general public. Category 2 facilities are inspected more frequently than Category 1 (Low Risk) operations but less frequently than Category 3 (High Risk) operations serving highly susceptible populations.

How are violations cited in Arkansas food service inspections?

ADH inspectors cite each violation with a specific section reference from Ark. Admin. Code 007.04.21-003 (effective October 24, 2022). Arkansas uses its own section numbering, which is not identical to FDA Food Code model ordinance section numbers. Each corrective action notice must reference the specific Arkansas code provision.

How does Arkansas food code differ from the FDA Food Code?

Arkansas adopted Ark. Admin. Code 007.04.21-003 effective October 24, 2022, which incorporates food safety requirements from the FDA Food Code framework but uses Arkansas's own section numbering. The section numbers in the Arkansas rules are not a direct one-to-one match with FDA Food Code section numbers, so operators should reference the Arkansas code directly.

Where can I see Arkansas restaurant inspection results?

Arkansas inspection results are publicly available at healthy.arkansas.gov. Consumers and operators can search for licensed food service establishment inspection reports by facility name or location.
