Arkansas Food Safety

Arkansas Food Code: A Complete Guide for Restaurant Operators

Arkansas uses a risk-category system that determines how often your restaurant is inspected and how violations are documented. Here is how the Arkansas Department of Health categorizes food service operations, what inspectors check, and how violations are cited with specific regulatory code references.

How Arkansas Food Inspections Work

Food safety inspections in Arkansas are conducted by the Arkansas Department of Health (ADH), Environmental Health Services. County offices carry out inspections statewide under state-level oversight and standardization. Full results are publicly accessible at healthy.arkansas.gov.

Arkansas assigns every licensed food service establishment to one of three risk categories: Category 1 (Low), Category 2 (Medium), or Category 3 (High). The risk category is determined by the type of food operations performed, the population served, and the complexity of food handling at the facility.

Most full-service restaurants fall into Category 2 (Medium Risk). This includes operations that cook raw animal proteins to order, hold TCS foods, and serve the general public. Category 2 facilities are inspected at a higher base frequency than Category 1 (Low Risk) operations such as convenience stores selling only prepackaged food.

A distinguishing feature of Arkansas inspections is that violations are cited with specific regulatory code references. Rather than just noting "cold holding failure," an ADH inspector cites the exact code provision -- for example, ADH Rules and Regulations Pertaining to Restaurant Operations, the relevant section number -- alongside the violation description. This means corrective action documents must reference the specific rule, not just the general category.

All routine inspections are unannounced. Re-inspections for critical violations or compliance failures are also unannounced.

Arkansas Risk Categories

ADH classifies food service establishments by risk level. The risk category determines inspection frequency and the level of regulatory scrutiny applied during each visit. Most full-service restaurants are Category 2.

Category 1
Low Risk
Operations that handle only commercially pre-packaged foods or do not handle TCS foods. Examples include convenience stores that sell only sealed products or facilities with minimal food handling. Inspected least frequently because the risk of foodborne illness from these operations is lower.
Category 2
Medium Risk -- Most Restaurants
Operations that cook raw animal proteins, hold TCS foods hot or cold, and serve the general public. This category covers the large majority of full-service restaurants, fast food operations, and food trucks in Arkansas. Inspected at a higher base frequency than Category 1, with unannounced routine and follow-up visits.
Category 3
High Risk
Operations that serve highly susceptible populations such as nursing home residents, hospital patients, daycare children, or perform specialized food processes such as smoking, curing, or reduced-oxygen packaging. Inspected most frequently. Compliance requirements are stricter and enforcement responses to violations are more rapid.

Most Common Violations in Arkansas Restaurant Inspections

ADH inspectors cite each violation with a specific regulatory code reference. These are the violation categories most frequently found in Arkansas food service inspections, particularly for Category 2 operations.

01
Temperature control failures (cold and hot holding)TCS foods held above 41°F in cold storage or below 135°F in hot holding are the most frequently cited violations in Arkansas. ADH inspectors probe temperatures directly. Each violation is cited with the specific section of the ADH food code addressing temperature control for safety foods.
02
Improper cooling of cooked foodsArkansas follows the FDA two-stage cooling requirement: 135°F to 70°F within 2 hours, then 70°F to 41°F within the next 4 hours. Large batches of rice, beans, soups, and proteins that are not cooled in shallow pans or with ice baths frequently fail this standard. Inspectors check cooling logs and probe foods in coolers.
03
Employee hygiene and handwashingBare-hand contact with ready-to-eat foods, failure to wash hands between raw protein handling and other food prep, and handwashing sinks that are inaccessible or stocked without soap and paper towels. Each finding is cited with the specific handwashing or personal hygiene code provision in the ADH regulations.
04
Cross-contamination from raw protein storageRaw meats, poultry, or seafood stored above or adjacent to ready-to-eat foods in walk-ins and reach-ins. ADH inspectors check cooler organization on every routine inspection. Violations cite the specific storage hierarchy provision from Arkansas food service regulations.
05
Date labeling and discard complianceReady-to-eat TCS foods prepared on site and held beyond 24 hours must carry a preparation date and consume-by date within 7 days. Missing labels, incorrect dates, and foods past their discard date are common findings. Inspectors check every labeled and unlabeled container in refrigerated storage.
06
Sanitizing solution concentration and equipmentChlorine or quaternary ammonium sanitizers outside the required concentration range, and multiuse food-contact surfaces that are not sanitized between uses. Inspectors test sanitizer solutions during the visit. Each violation cites the specific equipment sanitation or chemical sanitizer provision in the ADH code.
