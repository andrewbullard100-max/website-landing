Louisiana Food Code: A Complete Guide for Restaurant Operators

How the Louisiana Department of Health inspects restaurants, how demerit scoring works, the four types of inspections you may receive, and what violations get cited most often across the state's 32,000 licensed food operations.

How Louisiana Food Inspections Work

Food safety inspections in Louisiana are conducted by the Louisiana Department of Health (LDH), which oversees approximately 32,000 retail food operations across the state. The governing regulation is the Louisiana Sanitary Code, LAC Title 51, Part XXIII, which incorporates FDA Food Code provisions adapted for Louisiana's specific conditions.

Louisiana uses a demerit-based scoring system, meaning a perfect inspection starts at zero demerits. Each violation found during the inspection adds demerit points, with critical violations adding more demerits than non-critical violations. The final score reflects total demerits, not a percentage -- a lower number is better.

Inspection frequency is risk-based. Operations are assigned a risk category depending on the type of food handled, the preparation methods used, and the population served. A full-service restaurant handling raw proteins and cooking to order for the general public falls into a higher risk category and is inspected more frequently than a pre-packaged food retailer.

Inspection results are public and available through LDH's online dashboard. Operators should check their own facility's inspection history to understand patterns before a routine inspection occurs.

Louisiana's Four Inspection Types

LDH conducts four distinct types of inspections. Each serves a different purpose and triggers different compliance requirements. Operators should understand all four, not just the routine visit.

Routine Inspection
The standard unannounced inspection conducted on the risk-based schedule. Inspectors evaluate the full scope of the Louisiana Sanitary Code, from food temperatures and storage to equipment sanitation and employee hygiene. This is the inspection that determines the facility's demerit score on record.
Re-Inspection
Triggered when critical violations are found during a routine inspection that were not corrected before the inspector left. The re-inspection focuses on whether those specific violations have been corrected. Failure to correct critical violations at re-inspection can result in permit suspension or closure.
Pre-Opening Inspection
Required before a new food establishment can open or before a previously closed facility can reopen. Inspectors verify that the physical plant, equipment, and operational procedures meet Louisiana Sanitary Code requirements before any food service begins.
Complaint Inspection
Triggered by a complaint filed with LDH about a specific establishment. Complaint inspections are unannounced and can occur at any time -- including during a recent routine inspection cycle. High complaint volume increases scrutiny on a facility across all inspection types.

Most Common Violations in Louisiana Restaurant Inspections

Critical violations in Louisiana's demerit system carry higher point values because they directly contribute to conditions that can cause foodborne illness. These are the categories where LDH inspectors most consistently find violations.

01
Temperature abuse of TCS foodsCold-held foods above 41°F or hot-held foods below 135°F are critical violations in Louisiana's demerit system, carrying higher point deductions. Louisiana's climate makes temperature control even more critical -- ambient temperatures in kitchens push equipment harder, especially in the summer months.
02
Improper cooling proceduresFoods cooled too slowly from 135°F to 70°F (must reach 70°F within 2 hours) or from 70°F to 41°F (must reach 41°F within an additional 4 hours). Large batches of cooked proteins, gumbos, and soups are common problem points. Shallow pans, ice baths, and blast chillers are the correct approach.
03
Handwashing and personal hygieneEmployees not washing hands between tasks, after handling raw proteins, or after touching their face or phone. LDH inspectors observe behavior directly during the inspection. Bare-hand contact with ready-to-eat foods is a critical violation.
04
Cross-contamination from raw proteinsRaw meats, poultry, or seafood stored above or adjacent to ready-to-eat foods. Louisiana's cuisine frequently involves raw proteins at multiple stations, which creates more cross-contamination risk points. Each raw protein category must be segregated by final cooking temperature.
05
Insect and pest evidenceEvidence of rodents, cockroaches, or flies in food preparation or storage areas is a critical violation that can trigger immediate closure. Louisiana's climate creates year-round pest pressure. Regular pest control contracts and documentation are the baseline expectation.
06
Date marking and labelingReady-to-eat TCS foods prepared on site and held longer than 24 hours must be date-marked and discarded within 7 days. LDH inspectors check every container in the cooler. Unlabeled containers and items past their discard date are consistently among the highest-frequency violations found statewide.
